Learning plan



Learning a new language takes time and practice, so don't get discouraged if you don't see results right away. Keep practicing , be patient and you will gradually improve your English skills over time. Here are the steps I recommend you to follow in order to learn effectively.

1-Find your "why": Think of what drives you to learn English and remind yourself of it every time because that will fill you with energy to go after your goal.

2-Start with the basics: Begin with the basics of English vocabulary , pronunciation and grammar. This will create a strong foundation for further language development.

3-Practice listening and reading: Listen to English podcasts or songs , watch videos and reed books to see how the words and expressions you learned are used in daily life conversations. You will understand most of what you read or hear if you did the previous step and you can add up to what you have learned by searching for the meaning of the words that are new to you.

4-Practice speaking : You can practice speaking English every day with a native speaker or a language partner. You can also write in English and get feedback from someone who knows the language very well or AI.

Moreover , you can label the objects in your house so that you see them each day to remember them.

Varieties of English



English is a global language spoken in many different countries around the world with a number of regional dialects. Each variety of English has a different vocabulary, spelling and pronunciation.
The varieties of English with the largest number of native speakers are American English and British English. The vocabulary of these two has almost become the same since Britons are using more American words because they have been influenced by American films and songs.
The words in this website are spelled in American English and you might find some British words in the vocabulary part so that you could understand them if you hear people using them.
The other major varieties of English are Canadian English, Australian English, New Zealand English, South African English and Indian English. Here is a list of some words that are different in British and American English in case you want to look at the difference.

Phrasal verbs are combinations of a verb and one or more particles (typically prepositions or adverbs) that together create a new meaning.

Types of Phrasal Verbs Definition Examples
Literal The meaning of the phrasal verb = meaning of the verb + meaning of the particle. Go out, Run away
Figurative It has a totally different meaning from the verb and the particle. Give up
Separable The object can come between the verb and the particle or be placed after the particle. Turn on the lights. or Turn the lights on.
Non-separable The object must come after the particle; it cannot come between the verb and the particle. Look after your sister.

    The subjunctive mood:

      

    We use the subjunctive mainly in conditional clauses or after verbs or expressions like: suggest, recommend, demand, insist, ask, require, important that, necessary that, wish, if I were, as if, as though. And a common phrase using the present subjonctif is "So be it!" and after "It’s time" comes the subjunctive past to suggests urgency or that something must have been done earlier.
    Its conjugation is similar to the indicative, except that it does not change according to the subject. In the present, the verb keeps its infinitive form for all subjects (Ex: I suggest that he work hard.), and in the past simple, the verb “to be” is conjugated as “were” for all subjects (Ex: I wish I were taller.).


     

    The conditional mood:

      

    The conditional has a condition clause (introduced by words of condition like if, when, as long as, on condition that, considering that, whenever, supposing, unless, ...) and a result clause (introduced by modals like would, could, ...) and their order can be switched.

    Active and passive voice:

      

    Sentences can be written in either active or passive voice. In active voice, the subject performs the action, while in passive voice, the subject receives the action. The passive voice requires a direct object that becomes the subject of the passive sentence. So the verbs that are intransitive cannot naturally be made passive.

    Sentences containing 1 object:


    Form Subject Verb Object
    Active voice Sara writes the lesson.
    Passive voice The lesson is written by Sara.
    Rule of the Passive voice Object The verb "To be" in the tense of the verb + past participle of the verb by + Subject

    Sentences containing 2 objects:


    Form Subject Verb Object 2 Object 1

    Rule of the Passive voice Object 1 The verb "To be" in the tense of the verb + past participle of the verb Object 2 by + Subject
